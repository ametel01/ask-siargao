# Ask Siargao and Bruno: Pre-Product PMF, Go-to-Market, Traction, and Brand Strategy

## Core principle

A prototype can have technical value and intellectual property, but we should operate as though neither company has meaningful commercial value until people repeatedly use it, recommend it, or pay for it.

Product-market-fit work does **not** need to wait for a finished product. Waiting until the products are “ready” would be a mistake.

## The pre-product objective

Before building much more, we need to accumulate four kinds of evidence:

| Evidence | What it proves |
|---|---|
| **Problem evidence** | A specific audience has an important recurring problem |
| **Demand evidence** | People take action—not merely compliment the idea |
| **Value evidence** | We can manually or partially deliver a useful outcome |
| **Distribution evidence** | We can repeatedly reach similar customers at reasonable cost |

The early commercial loop should be:

```text
Find people → understand their problem → offer an outcome
→ deliver it manually → observe value → request payment
→ identify repetition → automate only the repeated parts
```

The product should emerge from this loop—not precede it.

# 1. Define a narrow initial customer and urgent problem

We need a precise first market for each product.

## Ask Siargao

### Initial customer

> A first-time Siargao visitor arriving within the next 60 days who must make an expensive or difficult trip decision.

Potential high-intent decisions:

- Where to stay
- Whether Siargao is suitable during their travel dates
- How weather or surf conditions affect plans
- General Luna versus quieter locations
- Airport and island transportation
- Surfing options for their ability
- Whether an activity or itinerary is realistic
- What requires advance booking

This is better than targeting “anyone interested in Siargao.” Immediate travelers have urgency, context, and consequential decisions.

## Bruno

### Initial customer

> A solo SaaS founder with paying customers who personally handles sales, support, product work, and launches.

The initial problem should be narrower than “run your company.” For example:

> Important revenue, customer, and product follow-ups are scattered across Gmail, Stripe, GitHub, and analytics—and the founder discovers them too late.

The first promise could be:

> Bruno reviews your business every morning and gives you the five highest-value actions, with responses already prepared.

That is understandable and testable before the full Business Graph exists.

# 2. Sell the outcome before automating it

We should create **concierge versions** of both products.

A concierge service is not fake. It is a controlled way to discover which parts deserve automation.

## Ask Siargao concierge beta

Recruit travelers and offer:

> “Send us the Siargao decision you’re currently struggling with. We’ll return a source-backed Reality Check covering what to keep, change, avoid, and confirm.”

The product can assist, but we manually review early answers.

This lets us learn:

- Which decisions people actually bring
- What information they provide initially
- What evidence earns trust
- Which sources are consistently needed
- Which answers change decisions
- What they would pay for
- Whether they return with another question

We should not ask merely, “Was this useful?”

Ask instead:

- What were you going to do before receiving this?
- What will you do now?
- Did you change accommodation, dates, transport, or itinerary?
- What would have happened without this answer?
- Would you have paid $5, $10, or $20 for it?
- Who else planning Siargao would need this?

## Bruno concierge pilot

Before implementing the new Bruno architecture, recruit 3–5 solo SaaS founders and manually provide:

- Business-system intake
- Daily Action Inbox
- Lead and customer follow-up detection
- Drafted actions
- Weekly CEO review
- Outcome report

Behind the scenes, we can combine scripts, spreadsheets, existing AI tools, and manual analysis.

The founder should experience:

> “Bruno found something important and helped close it.”

They do not need to know whether the Business Graph is fully automated.

This reveals:

- Which systems founders will connect
- Which signals matter
- Which actions they trust
- Which approvals become repetitive
- Which outcomes are measurable
- How much onboarding and support costs us
- Whether the product is worth paying for

# 3. Ask for commitment early

Praise is weak evidence. Commitment is evidence.

We should move prospects through an evidence ladder:

| Commitment | Strength |
|---|---:|
| Says the idea sounds useful | Very weak |
| Agrees to an interview | Weak |
| Introduces us to another prospect | Moderate |
| Gives real data or a real trip decision | Good |
| Connects a business account | Strong |
| Uses the result to make a decision | Strong |
| Returns without prompting | Very strong |
| Pays for continued access | Best early evidence |

For Bruno especially, we should request payment during the concierge stage. It can be a discounted founding-customer price, but it should be real.

## Possible Bruno pilot

> **Founding Operator Pilot — $49–99/month**  
> Daily Action Inbox, three connected systems, weekly CEO review, direct founder support, and influence over the product.

## Possible Ask Siargao offer

> **Founding Traveler Reality Check — free for the first decision, paid bundle for continued planning**

We can test multiple offer structures without turning on Trip Pass checkout prematurely. Payment availability remains governed by the existing launch controls.

# 4. Build an audience while learning

Brand recognition should come from repeated usefulness around a focused problem—not generic startup announcements.

## Ask Siargao’s pre-launch content

Create content around real traveler decisions:

- “General Luna or Pacifico?”
- “Is September a bad time to visit Siargao?”
- “Where should a beginner surfer stay?”
- “What changes when it rains for four days?”
- “Do you need to rent a scooter?”
- “Which plans require confirmation rather than assumption?”
- “What most Siargao itineraries get wrong”

Every piece should:

1. Answer a specific high-intent question.
2. Show honest uncertainty.
3. Cite governed local evidence.
4. Invite the reader to request a personalized Reality Check.
5. Capture email or beta interest.

This builds both search visibility and product-learning data.

## Bruno’s pre-launch content

Build authority around operating a solo SaaS company:

- Weekly solo-SaaS operating review template
- What a founder should check every morning
- How unanswered leads disappear
- How to connect customer complaints to GitHub work
- Why agent infrastructure is becoming commoditized
- Business Graph examples
- Action Inbox demonstrations
- Measuring an AI operator by revenue and time—not tokens
- Anonymized concierge-pilot results

The content should demonstrate Bruno’s operating philosophy before the software implements all of it.

A strong recurring series could be:

> **“What Bruno would have noticed this week.”**

Each post shows a realistic business event, why it matters, the proposed action, and how the result would be measured.

# 5. Recruit design partners—not generic beta testers

“Beta testers” often provide superficial UI feedback. We need **design partners** with real problems and active stakes.

## Ask Siargao design partners

Two categories:

### Travelers

- Traveling within 60 days
- Have unresolved planning decisions
- Agree to share whether advice changed their plans
- Participate in a short follow-up

### Local distribution partners

- Accommodation operators
- Surf schools
- Coworking spaces
- Property managers
- Tour and transport providers
- Travel creators

Partner proposition:

> “Give your guests access to independent, source-backed Siargao decision support. We’ll learn which pre-arrival questions create the most support burden and help answer them.”

A partner providing ten relevant travelers is more valuable than ten random signups.

## Bruno design partners

Look for founders who:

- Have existing customers or active trials
- Personally manage email, support, releases, and sales
- Use Gmail, Stripe, GitHub, and analytics
- Publicly discuss overload or missed follow-ups
- Will connect real systems
- Agree to a weekly review
- Can measure at least one business outcome
- May pay if value is demonstrated

We should avoid founders who mainly want to experiment with AI agents. They are attracted to technology, not necessarily Bruno’s business outcome.

# 6. Start distribution as experiments

We should not create a giant marketing plan. We should run small, measured channel experiments.

## Ask Siargao initial channel tests

| Channel | Initial experiment |
|---|---|
| Traveler communities | Give 20 useful answers and invite relevant people to a Reality Check |
| Search content | Publish 5 high-intent decision pages |
| Accommodation partners | Contact 20 carefully selected operators |
| Creators | Offer 10 Siargao creators personalized planning examples |
| Referral | Ask every successful beta traveler for one introduction |
| QR distribution | Test one accommodation or coworking-location QR page |

## Bruno initial channel tests

| Channel | Initial experiment |
|---|---|
| Direct founder outreach | Research and contact 30 highly qualified solo SaaS founders |
| Founder communities | Conduct 10 problem interviews without pitching immediately |
| Build-in-public accounts | Identify founders visibly handling support and sales themselves |
| Product Hunt alumni | Contact recent solo/tiny-team launchers |
| Content-led outreach | Send a relevant Bruno operating teardown, not a generic sales message |
| Referrals | Ask every interviewed founder to introduce one similar founder |

Each experiment should have a finite batch and a decision rule.

Example:

```text
Channel: Personalized email to solo SaaS founders
Batch: 30
Target: 8 replies
Qualified interviews: 5
Pilot starts: 2
Paid conversion target: 1

If fewer than 3 qualified replies:
Change segment, problem, or message before sending another batch.
```

# 7. Build traction instrumentation now

We should define traction before launch so we do not optimize vanity metrics.

## Ask Siargao metrics

### North-star candidate

> Number of traveler decisions meaningfully changed by a Reality Check.

Supporting metrics:

- Qualified visitors
- Reality Checks requested
- Reality Checks completed
- Time to useful answer
- Reported decision changes
- Second questions
- Seven-day return rate
- Referral rate
- Partner-originated travelers
- Willingness to pay
- Paid conversion when authorized
- Cost per answer
- Incorrect or stale-evidence incidents

A homepage visit is not traction. A traveler changing a real decision and referring another traveler is.

## Bruno metrics

### North-star candidate

> Number of verified, valuable business loops closed for active founders.

Supporting metrics:

- Qualified founders interviewed
- Systems connected
- Time to first useful action
- Actions proposed
- Actions approved
- Actions completed
- Outcomes verified
- Weekly retained founders
- Hours reportedly saved
- Revenue influenced
- Churn recovered
- Founder interruptions avoided
- Pilot conversion
- Monthly retention
- Support hours per customer
- AI and infrastructure cost per customer

A connected Gmail account is not traction. Bruno detecting an at-risk customer, preparing an intervention, and helping retain the account is.

# 8. Maintain a traction evidence repository

We should systematically collect:

- Interview notes
- Exact customer language
- Repeated pain patterns
- Rejections and objections
- Before-and-after outcomes
- Screenshots or anonymized examples
- Customer testimonials
- Referral behavior
- Pricing reactions
- Cohort retention
- Acquisition-channel performance
- Support burden
- Unit economics

This becomes useful for:

- Product decisions
- Homepage copy
- Sales conversations
- Case studies
- Partnership pitches
- Investor materials
- Acquisition due diligence

An investor or acquirer will care much more about:

> “Eight of ten design partners use Bruno weekly, five pay, and it closes an average of 11 measurable business loops per month”

than:

> “Bruno supports four agent runtimes and 1,000 integrations.”

# 9. Build brand recognition around a category promise

We should choose a consistent idea each brand can own.

## Ask Siargao

Potential category position:

> **The honest decision layer for Siargao travel.**

Brand associations:

- Current rather than generic
- Evidence-backed
- Honest about uncertainty
- Independent
- Locally governed
- Helps travelers decide, not merely browse

## Bruno

Potential category position:

> **The operating system for a one-person company.**

Or, for immediate customer acquisition:

> **Your solo SaaS business operator.**

Brand associations:

- Proactive
- Understands business state
- Closes open loops
- Asks only when needed
- Operates under clear policies
- Measures business outcomes

We should repeat these ideas consistently rather than changing positioning weekly.

# What not to do

Before product readiness, avoid:

- Building dozens of integrations
- Building generic “AI agent” capabilities
- Collecting a large unqualified waitlist
- Posting broadly without a conversion path
- Offering permanent free access
- Measuring followers, impressions, or signups alone
- Asking users which features they want
- Spending heavily on branding or paid advertising
- Automating workflows we have not manually validated
- Calling beta interest product-market fit
- Treating one successful customer as repeatable demand
- Chasing investors before we have customer evidence

# A practical 30-day program

## Week 1 — Positioning and research infrastructure

### Both projects

- Write one narrow ideal-customer profile
- Define the first painful job
- Create interview scripts
- Create a shared traction CRM
- Define evidence and metrics
- Prepare concise landing-page offers
- Prepare outreach templates
- Establish a weekly growth review

### Targets

- Ask Siargao: identify 50 qualified travelers or distribution sources
- Bruno: identify 50 qualified solo SaaS founders

## Week 2 — Customer discovery

- Conduct 10 Ask Siargao traveler interviews
- Conduct 10 Bruno founder interviews
- Record exact language and pain frequency
- Ask for introductions
- Test willingness to provide real data
- Avoid feature pitching during the first half of each interview

Decision at end of week:

- Which problem is most urgent?
- Who experiences it most frequently?
- What workaround do they use?
- What has the problem already cost them?
- Who will commit to a concierge trial?

## Week 3 — Concierge delivery

### Ask Siargao

- Deliver 10 reviewed Reality Checks
- Measure whether decisions changed
- Request referrals and testimonials
- Recruit 1–2 local distribution partners

### Bruno

- Onboard 3 founders
- Produce daily or scheduled Action Inboxes
- Deliver a weekly CEO review
- Track approvals, completed actions, and measurable outcomes
- Ask at least one founder to begin paying

## Week 4 — Evaluate and tighten

For each project:

- Aggregate objections
- Identify repeated workflows
- Calculate delivery and support cost
- Identify the strongest channel
- Publish one evidence-backed case study
- Revise positioning
- Stop low-signal channels
- Select only the product work necessary to improve the validated loop
- Set the next 30-day target

# The milestones that create real company value

## Stage 1 — Problem validation

- Repeated pain appears in interviews
- Customers already use imperfect workarounds
- The problem has urgency or financial consequences

## Stage 2 — Solution validation

- Customers provide real inputs
- Delivered outputs change behavior
- Users return and refer others

## Stage 3 — Revenue validation

- Multiple unrelated customers pay
- They pay without excessive persuasion
- Price exceeds variable delivery cost

## Stage 4 — Retention validation

- Customers repeatedly use the product
- Value survives beyond novelty
- Churn reasons are understood

## Stage 5 — Distribution validation

- One channel consistently produces qualified customers
- Conversion can be measured
- Acquisition is economically sustainable

## Stage 6 — Repeatable growth

- Onboarding is reproducible
- Delivery is increasingly automated
- Retention and margins are stable
- Growth does not depend entirely on Alex’s personal labor

That progression—not code volume—is what creates defensible value.

# Immediate recommendation

Begin with **two concierge cohorts simultaneously**:

1. **Ten travelers with consequential Siargao decisions**
2. **Three solo SaaS founders receiving a manually assisted Bruno Action Inbox**

Do not wait for either product to feel complete. These cohorts will tell us what to build, what to remove, what language converts, where distribution exists, and whether anyone will pay.
